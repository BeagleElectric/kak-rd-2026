Take the enquiry

Tell them

Four things

First payment comes out immediately. Every recurring payment after that comes out 4 days after the rental renews — so if they return the trailer on the anniversary, there's time to cancel before it pulls.

STEP 11

Late payments

✓

You are the decision maker — act like it

You set the deadline, you hold it, and you cancel the rental if it passes. Don't write “as instructed by our manager” or “our manager would like” — it tells the customer you're only a messenger and the real decision sits somewhere else, so he pushes past you. Say I. Bring Greg in only for the genuinely hard ones.

!

Never soften the ending

Don't finish a demand with “we truly hope we can avoid this” or “we've been understanding of your situation.” That reads as we don't want to do this and we've bent before — it's an invitation to push for more time. State the consequence and stop.

!

Always quote the balance and the next invoice date

Not “you're behind.” Say “$7,120 today, and another $3,780 goes on September 5.” Customers track what they've paid, not what they've been charged — that's why they say “I should be caught up.” The forward number is what makes it real.

Days lateAction
Same dayPayment declines — text the customer. Usually an expired card or changed account.
1–5Friendly reminder, text then email.
10Firm notice. Remind them we can end the rental at 15 days and that unpaid accounts go to collections.
15You now have the right to end the rental. Say so — but hold off, bank payments can take a week to show.
18Final warning. Name the termination date.
20No payment and no contact — terminate. Not a judgment call. If they're talking to you and on a plan, that's different.
After returnBalance owing goes to final demand, then collections. Tell Greg before referring.
!

Two separate conversations

Chasing money and demanding the trailer back go in separate messages. Never say that paying stops us taking the trailer.

The test: can this account recover?

Compare what they pay in a month against the monthly rent. That one comparison tells you whether to keep working with them or get the unit back.

What you seeWhat it means
Paying more than the rentCatching up. Let the plan run.
Paying about the rentStanding still. Arrears aren't shrinking — needs a plan with real numbers.
Paying less than the rentGoing backwards every month. This account cannot recover while they have the trailer. Get the unit back.
!

Paying something is not the same as recovering

A customer paying $2,500 a month against $3,780 of rent falls behind $1,280 every month — even though money arrives every month. It feels like progress and isn't. Do the arithmetic before you agree to anything.

Once it's terminated

A cancelled rental can be reinstated, but only on all four of these — and it's Greg's call, not yours:

  • Cleared funds for arrears, current month and any fees. Landed, not sent — in practice that means e-transfer or card.
  • Within 3 days of the termination notice
  • Greg approves
  • Card only going forward, or a bigger deposit

Any late payment after reinstatement is immediate termination with no second chance. Put that in writing when you reinstate.

A date is not a payment

When a customer misses a deadline and counter-offers a new date, the deadline does not move. The only thing that keeps a rental alive is money landing — today, and partial is fine. If nothing lands, cancel on the date you said you would. A deadline you move once is worth nothing afterwards.

!

Stop pulling after two failed attempts

Re-running a debit you know will fail just stacks $50 fees onto someone who already can't pay, and makes the balance harder to recover. After two, switch to e-transfer or card only and tell the customer that's what's happening.

REFERENCE B

Payment plans

Two standard plans. Offer one of these — don't invent terms on the phone.

!

The rule that makes these work

Every payment is pulled automatically from the account or card on file, set up in Rotessa or Converge the same day the plan is agreed. Never accept “I'll send you a thousand this week.” A promise to send money is what got the account here in the first place.

Plan A — trailer still out

The arrears have to clear before the next rent payment is due. Ongoing rent keeps running on top. That's a shorter window than most customers expect, so say it plainly at the start of the call.

PaymentWhenHow much
1Within 2 business days50% of the arrears
2At least 3 days before the next rent dateThe remaining 50%
Next rentIts normal dateFull monthly rent, as usual
!

If they can't do that, they can't afford the trailer

That's the honest read, and it's useful information. Don't stretch Plan A to fit — talk to Greg about ending the rental and getting the unit back, then deal with the balance under Plan B.

Plan B — trailer returned, balance owing

Rent has stopped, so the balance is fixed and there's room to be flexible. We'd rather have the money over three months than hand 25% to a collection agency.

BalanceStandard plan
Under $2,00025% now, then 2 monthly payments
$2,000 – $10,00025% now, then 3 monthly payments
Over $10,00025% now, then 3 monthly payments — ask Greg before agreeing anything longer

Greg can stretch this to 4 or 6 months on a bigger balance if the customer is straight with us. Ask — don't offer it yourself.

Setting one up
1

Work out the exact number first

Deposit applied, wash and damage charges in, any partial payments credited. The plan is built on that figure and you can't change it afterwards without redoing the whole thing.

2

Tell Greg before you offer it

Every plan needs his sign-off. Takes two minutes.

3

Send the Payment Plan Agreement through SignNow

Fill in the amount and the schedule before sending. Customer signs, you countersign.

Payment Plan Agreement
4

Set up every payment in Rotessa or Converge the same day

All of them, scheduled on the dates in the plan. Not one at a time as they come due — that's how they get forgotten.

Same day, all payments
5

Note it on the fleet board and diarize each date

Check each payment actually cleared during your Tuesday and Friday matching.

!

One miss and the plan is over

Any payment declined, returned or cancelled — or any ongoing rent missed — and the whole remaining balance is due at once. Go straight back to the escalation ladder at the stage you were at. Don't renegotiate a plan that has already failed once.
